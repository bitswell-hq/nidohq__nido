\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

The evaluation runs on a local Stellar network and on testnet. It measures
proving time and memory on desktop and phone-class devices; the on-chain cost
of verification in instructions, bytes and fees; and an attack suite covering
forged and replayed emails, a wrong account or key in the statement, stale or
malicious registry keys, a compromised inbox, provider header quirks, and
front-running.

\paragraph{On-chain cost.}
Each launch domain's recovery email was proved with Perch's pinned prover
and submitted as one guardian approval on Nido's integration-test stack,
with Perch's controller and every contract running from its WebAssembly.
\claimstatement{onchain-verification-cost}\claim{onchain-verification-cost}
The cost does not depend on the provider: the proof, its public inputs and
the circuit's size are the same for every email. The signed transaction is
dominated by the 16{,}224-byte proof inside the guardian's authorisation
entry.
\claimstatement{network-verification-cost}\claim{network-verification-cost}
\claimstatement{testnet-email-recovery}\claim{testnet-email-recovery}

\paragraph{Proving.}
\claimstatement{desktop-proving-cost}\claim{desktop-proving-cost}
\claimstatement{browser-proving-cost}\claim{browser-proving-cost}
A browser without cross-origin isolation proves on one thread; the recovery
origin's isolation headers let bb.js use one worker per core.

\paragraph{Attacks.}
The circuit-level attack suite is part of the implementation
(\S\ref{sec:impl}); its cases cover forged and replayed emails, a wrong
account or statement, and provider header quirks, against the circuit with
mocked provider emails.

Every attack class also runs on a local Stellar network.
