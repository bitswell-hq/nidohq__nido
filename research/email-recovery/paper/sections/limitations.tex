\section{Limitations and open questions}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Not built, not measured.} The production enclave is not built:
the binding to the Nitro secure module, TLS terminating inside it, the
recovery address's mail exchanger inside it with the domain's MTA-STS
policy, and a reproducible image build. The custodians, registry members and
attesters are not named. Every third party is a mock, so whether each
provider's outbound recovery mail matches its mock (its signing domains, its
signed block for long addresses, whether its Sent copy keeps the signature)
is not confirmed. Phones are not measured\claim{phone-proving-budget}, nor
votes against a registry root on a network. There is no external audit; the
system runs on testnet only, and mainnet waits for one. The model's
agreement with the code and the circuit's parsing are tested, not
proven\claim{model-code-agreement}.

\paragraph{The privacy claim's conditions.} \PrivacyClaim{} rests on A8,
A10 and A11, and nothing built detects a breach of either. Committing the
recovery domain's MX, MTA-STS policy and TLS key in the enclave-image
registry under its 14-day timelock, or publishing reproducible wallet builds,
would make one public; neither is built. The \texttt{.eml} import does not
rest on A10: the proof does not bind the recipient, so an email the user
sends to their own address and imports reaches no new party.

\paragraph{Inherent leakage.} The provider learns that its user sent a
recovery approval, and when. The blinded Subject hides which attempt, but
the vote lands seconds later: the browser proves in 2.81\,s on a desktop and
testnet confirmed the vote 1{,}715\,ms after
simulation\claims{browser-proving-cost,testnet-email-recovery}. A provider
watching the chain therefore links the email to its vote, and so to the
account, whenever few email votes land in that window, which can be one;
the same holds for an enrollment email and the guardian deployment that
follows it. Unlinkability from the provider is thus content-level only;
delaying submission within the statement's validity would widen the window
at the cost of recovery time. The sending service sees alert addresses and
codes; the alert host learns which accounts register contacts, and traffic
timing which have them.

\paragraph{Residual risks and burdens.} Under email plus delay, a phished
user with no device left cannot cancel, and an owner whose push lapsed while
alerts go to a stolen inbox learns of an attempt only by opening the wallet;
a second helper closes both. The email factor is the inbox and a 128-bit
code, so a lost code loses the factor; how often users keep it is unknown,
and storing it with the inbox's provider joins the two. The factor covers
losing every passkey device while the inbox stays reachable from a new one;
a provider that requires the lost device to sign in, or a new phone without
a mail app, defeats it, and an \texttt{.eml} import is hard on a phone. A
lowered target close time shortens existing email-plus-delay members until
they are rebuilt (A9), and a flood of sealed-inbound registrations from many
clients can fill the vault, leaving the \texttt{.eml} path.

\paragraph{Open questions.} Appendix~\ref{app:open} lists every decision
that remains open, with its current setting and alternatives: the pin's
semantics and lifetime; the default allowed actions, including whether an
inbox thief's approvals of cancellations should be alerted; where the account
code is kept; the registry's grace, revocation threshold and members; the
alert code's derivation, where a key from the passkey's PRF output would give
an alert the phisher cannot predict; an on-chain enrollment proof; guardian
deployment and who pays on mainnet; reading the push channel's state on
every open; larger bounds for custom domains; custodians, the production
enclave and the image registry's governance; outages longer than RPC
retention; codes for accounts without an email guardian; what the alert host
learns; failover duplicates; probe visibility; a lowered close time; each
provider's sender authentication; and who funds the keeper.
