\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{zkEmail's recovery stack.} The zkEmail
project~\cite{zkemail-docs,zkemail-verify,zkemail-nr} proves statements about
DKIM-signed email in zero knowledge, and its libraries have been audited
publicly~\cite{audit-zksecurity-2024,audit-yacademy,audit-zellic-2024,audit-matterlabs-2024,audit-veridise-2024,audit-consensys-2024}.
Its email wallet~\cite{suegami-email-wallet} and account-recovery
modules~\cite{zkemail-recovery} make email guardians of ERC-4337~\cite{erc4337}
and ERC-7579~\cite{erc7579} accounts with a weighted threshold and a delay;
each guardian's address derives from a hash of the email address and an
account code, as $\Cm$ here does, and a guardian approves by replying to a
request email whose command names the account. In its SDK~\cite{zkemail-tx-builder}
a relayer receives the email, generates the proof and stores users' account
codes. Its \texttt{UserOverrideableDKIMRegistry}~\cite{zkemail-user-overrideable-registry}
pairs a main authorizer, whose key additions take effect after a fixed
delay, with per-user keys that take effect at once and per-user revocation and
reactivation; the contract is upgradeable by its owner, who can also replace
the authorizer. The ideas shared with this work are the account-code
commitment, the email guardian under a delay, and a delayed registry with a
per-user path. What is new here: the email binds the recovery protocol's own
statement digest, blinded from the provider, not a readable command; the
proof is made in the user's browser, so no relayer receives the email or the
code; the registry is immutable, its additions need a multisig threshold and
wait 72 hours of close time after DNS attestation or 7 days without, and an
emergency threshold revokes; the per-account pin is salted, expiring, and
fixed with the guardian, so changing it passes the recovery protocol's own
reconfiguration gate; the composition with an unchanged recovery protocol is
machine-checked; and the header parser is written against a stated grammar,
hardened per public issue class, and checked against reference
implementations on real mail.

\paragraph{Credentials from existing infrastructure.} zkLogin~\cite{zklogin}
and Aptos's keyless accounts~\cite{aptos-aip61} make an OpenID Connect token
the account's key, hiding the off-chain identity; Aptos names a DKIM proof as
a possible recovery path. An OIDC path here would be a second way to produce
the email factor, never a factor independent of the same provider's DKIM.
DECO~\cite{deco} and zk-creds~\cite{zkcreds} likewise turn an authority's
existing signature into a credential and inherit that authority's trust.
KeyForge~\cite{specter-keyforge} would publish expired DKIM keys so stolen
mail cannot be proven authentic; that turns registry and pin entries for
those keys into forgeable keys, and the registry's retirement and revocation
and the pin's lifetime are the levers such a policy would act on.

\paragraph{Guardian and server recovery.} Social recovery
wallets~\cite{buterin-social-recovery} let guardians replace the signing key;
Argent's guardians trigger a recovery the owner can cancel during a security
period~\cite{argent-spec}; Stellar's SEP-30 servers hold signing keys and
authenticate users by email or phone~\cite{sep30}. This design keeps their
delay and veto and removes the operator from the check.

\paragraph{Recovery and notifications in usable security.} Secret questions
are guessable and forgotten~\cite{schechter-secret-questions,bonneau-recovery};
trustees can vouch for a user but must resist impersonators of
that user~\cite{schechter-social-recovery}; users of recovery codes lose them
or hold codes that no longer work~\cite{holtervennhoff-recovery-codes}; and manual hijackers who take an
inbox use it to lock the owner out~\cite{bursztein-hijacking}, the inbox
thief of \S\ref{sec:threat}. Notification design changes behaviour at
scale~\cite{golla-2fa-notifications}, yet few users act on a notice of a
malicious sign-in~\cite{markert-login-notifications}, and repeated warnings
habituate~\cite{anderson-habituation}. These results motivate the
email-plus-delay floor, alerts that lead with cancelling on a passkey
device, silence on attempts that grant nothing, and a check of the recovery
code before it is needed; they also mark A12 and the user's behaviour as the
weakest links. Providers' sender authentication, which A1b needs, has been
measured to fail in several submission
paths~\cite{chen-composition-kills,shen-weak-links}.
